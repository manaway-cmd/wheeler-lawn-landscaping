Sprinkler Repair & Irrigation FAQ - Lee's Summit, MO

Straight answers to what Lee's Summit-area homeowners ask us most, about repair, winterizing, and spring start-up.

How much does sprinkler repair cost in Lee's Summit?

Most single-zone repairs, like a broken head or a stuck valve, run in the low hundreds including the trip. Bigger jobs, such as a mainline break or a controller replacement, cost more. We give a firm price after we see the system, not before.

When should I winterize my sprinkler system in the Kansas City area?

Before the first hard freeze, which typically lands mid-to-late October in this part of Missouri. Frost dates shift year to year, so we watch the forecast and book by neighborhood rather than a single fixed calendar date.

When should I turn my sprinkler system on in spring?

Once overnight lows are reliably staying above freezing, which is usually early-to-mid April around Lee's Summit. Turning the water back on before then risks a late frost catching water in the lines again, undoing the point of winterizing.

What happens if I don't winterize before a freeze?

Water trapped in the pipes, valves, and backflow device expands as it freezes and cracks the fittings where you can't see it happen. You often don't find out until spring start-up, when a cracked line means a repair bill instead of a routine turn-on.

How long does a winterization blow-out take?

Usually 20 to 30 minutes for a typical residential system. We shut off the water supply, connect an air compressor, and blow each zone clear in sequence, then drain and insulate the backflow device.

Can I run my sprinklers year-round if I skip winterization?

No, not in this climate. Kansas City gets hard freezes every winter, and any water left in irrigation pipe or a backflow assembly will freeze at some point in the season. Skipping the blow-out isn't a shortcut, it's a bet against the weather.

Why is one zone not turning on?

Usually a stuck or failed valve, a wiring break between the controller and that valve, or a tripped breaker on the controller itself. We test the wire path with a multimeter before we dig, so we open ground only where the problem actually is.

What causes a sprinkler head to stop popping up?

Dirt or grass clippings jammed in the head, a cracked riser from a mower strike, or low pressure caused by a leak elsewhere on that zone. We pull the head, check the seat, and run the zone to see the actual pressure.

How do I tell if I have an underground leak?

A soggy patch that stays wet between waterings, a zone that runs but barely reaches the far heads, or a water bill that jumps with no other explanation. We pressure-test each zone to find which section is losing water.

Do you repair Hunter and Rain Bird systems?

Yes. Most systems in this area run Hunter, Rain Bird, or Toro components, and we carry common heads, nozzles, and valve parts for all three on the truck. If a controller needs a brand-specific part, we order it and let you know the wait.

How often should sprinkler heads be adjusted or replaced?

Check spray patterns every spring since heads shift from freeze-thaw movement, mower contact, and normal settling. Most heads last five to ten years before the internals wear out and the spray gets uneven or weak.

Do you offer same-week service calls?

During spring start-up season we book by the week rather than same-day, since everyone's system needs attention at once. Call or text early in the season for the shortest wait.

Is Wheeler Lawn & Landscaping licensed in Missouri?

Missouri has no state-level license for irrigation contractors. Some states do: Texas, for example, requires a state irrigator license. Here the work is handled through local city permitting and general contractor rules instead, so there is no state irrigation license number to look up for any Missouri company, including this one. The questions worth asking a contractor in this state are whether your job needs a city permit and whether they carry insurance.

How long has Wheeler been in business?

Public directories list Wheeler as serving the area since around 1999, about 27 years. That figure comes from directory listings rather than from the company, so treat it as directory-sourced. Ask on the phone if you want it confirmed firsthand.

Why does my lawn still look patchy even though the sprinklers are running?

Two causes come up most. One is the soil. Heavy clay is normal around Kansas City, and it takes water in slowly, so a single long run time can run off the surface before it reaches the roots. Splitting that same total time into two or three shorter cycles with a break between them often fixes it. The other is the equipment: a head that has sunk, tilted, gotten blocked by grass, or been fitted with the wrong nozzle leaves dry gaps no schedule can cover. We run each zone and watch it before changing anything, because the fix is different depending on which one it is.
