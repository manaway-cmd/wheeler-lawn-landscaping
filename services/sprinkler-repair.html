What's usually broken

Most repair calls in Lee's Summit come down to five things: a head cracked by a mower or trimmer, a valve that's stuck open or closed, a wire break between the controller and a zone, a leak in the mainline, or a controller that's lost its program after a power blip. Freeze damage from a missed winterization shows up as several of these at once every spring.

How we diagnose and fix it

  1. Call or text. Tell us what you're seeing - a dry zone, standing water, a head that won't retract.
  2. Zone test. We run every zone from the controller to see which ones fail and how.
  3. Pinpoint. Multimeter for wiring, pressure test for leaks, visual check for heads and risers.
  4. Repair. Most head, valve, and wiring repairs happen the same visit.
  5. Retest. We run the system again before we leave so you're not finding out it's still broken next week.

How long it takes

Head and valve repairs are usually a single visit, often under an hour once we've found the cause. Wire breaks and mainline leaks take longer to locate than to fix - expect one to three hours depending on how far we have to trace. Controller swaps are typically 30 to 45 minutes.

Frequently asked questions

Do you repair Hunter and Rain Bird systems?

Yes. Most systems in this area run Hunter, Rain Bird, or Toro components, and we carry common heads, nozzles, and valve parts for all three on the truck. If a controller needs a brand-specific part, we order it and let you know the wait.

Why is one zone not turning on?

Usually a stuck or failed valve, a wiring break between the controller and that valve, or a tripped breaker on the controller itself. We test the wire path with a multimeter before we dig, so we open ground only where the problem actually is.

What causes a sprinkler head to stop popping up?

Dirt or grass clippings jammed in the head, a cracked riser from a mower strike, or low pressure caused by a leak elsewhere on that zone. We pull the head, check the seat, and run the zone to see the actual pressure.

How do I tell if I have an underground leak?

A soggy patch that stays wet between waterings, a zone that runs but barely reaches the far heads, or a water bill that jumps with no other explanation. We pressure-test each zone to find which section is losing water.
